SYNOP Code Groups Explained

A comprehensive guide to understanding SYNOP meteorological observation codes

Start Learning

What is SYNOP?

SYNOP (surface synoptic observations) is a numerical code used for reporting weather observations made by manned and automated weather stations. The code is regulated by the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) and is used globally for weather forecasting and climate monitoring.

Each SYNOP report consists of several sections with specific groups that encode different meteorological parameters. Understanding these groups is essential for meteorologists and weather enthusiasts.

Note: SYNOP codes follow a strict format where each character or group of characters represents specific weather information.

YYGGiw IIiii iRiXhVV Nddff 1SnTTT 2SnTdTdTd 3PoPoPoPo 48hhh/PPPP 6RRRtR 7wwW1W2 8NhClCmCh 1SnTxTxTx 2SnTnTnTn 5SnPPP 8NsChshshs

Section 1: Identification & Time Data

Header Required

Identifies the type of SYNOP message and its priority.

Examples:
SMKN75 HKNC 230600 - Main SYNOP report
SIKN75 HKNC 230900 - Intermediate SYNOP report
SNKN75 HKNC 230700 - Hourly SYNOP report

Format: 5-character code indicating message type and priority.

Type of Station (AAXX) Required

Identifies the type of station and report.

Fixed value: AAXX for land stations

Format: A = Report from land station, A = Report made at a scheduled time, X = Included for completeness, X = Included for completeness

YYGGiw Required

Date, time, and wind indicator.

Example: 23182
23 = Day of month (23rd)
18 = Time in UTC (18:00)
4 = Wind indicator (units and averaging period)

Wind Indicator (iw):
0 = Wind in m/s, estimated
1 = Wind in m/s, measured by anemometer
3 = Wind in knots, estimated
4 = Wind in knots, measured by anemometer

IIiii Required

Station identification number.

Example: 63739
63 = Country or region code
739 = Station number within country/region

Each country has a specific block number assigned by WMO. For example, block 03 is for the United States, 07 for France, etc.

Section 2: Surface Weather Observations

iRiXhVV Required

Precipitation indicator, station operation, visibility.

Example: 32580
3 = Precipitation indicator (iR)
2 = Type of station and inclusion/omission of present and past wx(iX)()
5 = Height of base of cloud (h)
80 = Horizontal visibility (VV)

Components:
iR: Precipitation indicator (0-4)
iX: Type of station (1=manual, 2=automatic)
h: Height of base of lowest cloud (0-9)
VV: Horizontal visibility in km (00-99)

Nddff Required

Cloud cover, wind direction, and wind speed.

Example: 52315
5 = Total cloud cover (N)
23 = Wind direction in tens of degrees (dd)
15 = Wind speed (ff)

Components:
N: Total cloud cover (0-8, 9=sky obscured)
dd: True wind direction in tens of degrees (00-36)
ff: Wind speed (units depend on iw in YYGGiw)

1SnTTT Required

Air temperature data.

Example: 10125
1 = Group indicator (fixed)
0 = Sign of temperature (Sn)
125 = Temperature in tenths of degrees (TTT)

Components:
1: Fixed indicator for temperature group
Sn: Sign of temperature (0=positive, 1=negative)
TTT: Temperature in tenths of degrees Celsius

2SnTdTdTd Required

Dew point temperature data.

Example: 20095
2 = Group indicator (fixed)
0 = Sign of dew point (Sn)
095 = Dew point in tenths of degrees (TdTdTd)

Components:
2: Fixed indicator for dew point group
Sn: Sign of dew point (0=positive, 1=negative)
TdTdTd: Dew point in tenths of degrees Celsius

3PoPoPoPo Required

Station pressure at sea level.

Example: 38384
3 = Group indicator (fixed)
8384 = Station pressure in tenths of hPa (PoPoPoPo)

Components:
3: Fixed indicator for station pressure group
PoPoPoPo: Station pressure in tenths of hPa

If the pressure is less than 1000 hPa, 10 is added to the first digit (e.g., 998.5 hPa becomes 0998 then 10998).

4aPPP or 8hhh Required

Geopotential height.

Example: 48532
4 = Group indicator (fixed)
8 = isobaric pressure level
1532 = height of 850mb(geopotential height)

Components:
4: Fixed indicator for pressure group
a: isobaric pressure level
hhh: height of 850mb(geopotential height)

Characteristic (a) values:
0-3: Rising, 4: Steady, 5-8: Falling

5appp Required

Pressure change over 24 hours.

Example: 5012
5 = Group indicator (fixed)
0 = Sign of pressure change (a)
12 = Pressure change in tenths of hPa (ppp)

Components:
5: Fixed indicator for 24-hour pressure change
a: Sign of pressure change (8=positive, 9=negative)
ppp: Pressure change in tenths of hPa over 24 hours

6RRRtR Optional

Amount of precipitation.

Example: 60051
6 = Group indicator (fixed)
005 = Amount of precipitation (RRR)
1 = Duration period (t)

Components:
6: Fixed indicator for precipitation group
RRR: Amount of precipitation in mm
t: Duration period of precipitation measurement

7wwW1W2 Required

Present and past weather.

Example: 76186
7 = Group indicator (fixed)
61 = Present weather (ww)
8 = Past weather (W1)
6 = Past weather (W2)

Components:
7: Fixed indicator for weather group
ww: Present weather code (00-99)
W1: Past weather (1st period)
W2: Past weather (2nd period)

Weather codes describe conditions like rain, snow, fog, thunderstorms, etc.

8NhClCmCh Required

Cloud types and amounts.

Example: 82352
8 = Group indicator (fixed)
2 = Amount of low clouds (N)
3 = Type of low clouds (Cl)
5 = Type of medium clouds (Cm)
2 = Type of high clouds (Ch)

Components:
8: Fixed indicator for cloud group
Nh: Amount of low or middle clouds
Cl: Type of low clouds
Cm: Type of medium clouds
Ch: Type of high clouds

Section 3: Additional & Regional Data

1SnTxTxTx Optional

Maximum temperature over a period.

Example: 10225
1 = Group indicator (fixed)
0 = Sign of temperature (Sn)
225 = Maximum temperature in tenths of degrees (TxTxTx)

Components:
1: Fixed indicator for maximum temperature
Sn: Sign of temperature (0=positive, 1=negative)
TxTxTx: Maximum temperature in tenths of degrees Celsius

This group reports the highest temperature observed over a specified period (usually 12 or 24 hours).

2SnTnTnTn Optional

Minimum temperature over a period.

Example: 20085
2 = Group indicator (fixed)
0 = Sign of temperature (Sn)
085 = Minimum temperature in tenths of degrees (TnTnTn)

Components:
2: Fixed indicator for minimum temperature
Sn: Sign of temperature (0=positive, 1=negative)
TnTnTn: Minimum temperature in tenths of degrees Celsius

This group reports the lowest temperature observed over a specified period (usually 12 or 24 hours).

8NsChshshs Optional

Significant cloud layers.

Example: 82126
8 = Group indicator (fixed)
2 = Amount of cloud (Ns)
1 = Type of cloud (Ch)
26 = Height of cloud base (hh)

Components:
8: Fixed indicator for cloud layer group
Ns: Amount of cloud layer
Ch: Type of cloud
hh: Height of cloud base

This group can be repeated for multiple significant cloud layers.

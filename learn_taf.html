TAF — Terminal Aerodrome Forecast

Understanding Terminal Aerodrome Forecasts and Key Terms

What is a TAF?

A Terminal Aerodrome Forecast (TAF) is an aviation weather forecast for a specific aerodrome (airport), describing expected weather conditions within a radius of 4 NM (8 km) from the aerodrome reference point. It is coded in a concise alphanumeric format and follows ICAO/WMO standards.

TAFs are issued at regular intervals (usually every 6 hours) and typically cover 24 to 30 hours, depending on the country or region. Some high-traffic airports issue 9-hour short TAFs (TAF SHORT) to provide more frequent updates.

Structure of a TAF

The general format of a TAF report includes:

TAF CCCC YYGGggZ DDHH/DDHH dddff(f)KT VVVV w'w' NsNsNshshshs ...
  • TAF — Report identifier
  • CCCC — ICAO location indicator (4-letter airport code)
  • YYGGggZ — Date and time of issue (UTC)
  • DDHH/DDHH — Validity period (day/hour to day/hour)
  • dddffKT — Wind direction (° true) and speed (knots)
  • VVVV — Visibility (in meters or 9999 for ≥10km)
  • w’w’ — Weather phenomena (e.g. RA = rain, FG = fog)
  • NsNsNshshshs — Cloud amount, type, and height
  • Optional modifiers such as BECMG, TEMPO, PROB describe changes.

Key Change Indicators

BECMG (Becoming)

BECMG indicates a permanent or long-term change in weather conditions is expected to occur gradually, usually over a 2-hour period unless otherwise specified. Once the change is complete, the new condition will persist. 

Example: BECMG 1214 30015KT
→ Between 12:00 and 14:00 UTC, wind will gradually become 300° at 15 knots.

Key point: Change is gradual and lasting, not temporary.

TEMPO (Temporary)

TEMPO indicates that temporary fluctuations in weather are expected. Each fluctuation usually lasts less than 1 hour and in total covers less than half of the forecast period. 

Example: TEMPO 1014 2000 SHRA
→ Between 10:00 and 14:00 UTC, expect occasional showers reducing visibility to 2000 m,
   but only for short durations (temporary).

Key point: Weather changes are short-lived and intermittent.

PROB (Probability)

PROB30 or PROB40 indicates the probability (30% or 40%) of a weather event occurring during the specified period. Commonly used for low-confidence phenomena such as thunderstorms or fog. 

Example: PROB30 TEMPO 1822 TSRA
→ Between 18:00 and 22:00 UTC, there is a 30% chance of temporary thunderstorms with rain.

Key point: Adds uncertainty to the forecast. If combined with TEMPO, it means the event is both temporary and not certain.

Example of a Full TAF

TAF HKRE 220500Z 2206/2306 06012KT 9999 SCT020 BKN100 
     TEMPO 2209/2212 4000 SHRA BKN015 
     BECMG 2212/2214 09015KT 
     PROB30 TEMPO 2218/2222 2000 TSRA FEW020CB BKN021

Decoded:

  • Issued at 08:00 UTC on the 22nd for MOI AIR BASE, NAIROBI (HKRE).
  • Valid from 09:00 UTC on the 22nd to 12:00 UTC on the 23rd.
  • Initially: wind 060° at 12 kt, visibility 10 km+, scattered clouds at 2000 ft, broken at 10,000 ft.
  • TEMPO: Between 09–12 UTC, temporary showers reducing visibility to 4000 m, broken cloud at 1500 ft.
  • BECMG: Between 12–14 UTC, wind will gradually become 090° at 15 kt.
  • PROB30 TEMPO: Between 18–22 UTC, 30% chance of temporary thunderstorms with rain, FEW cumulonimbus at 2000 ft AND BROKEN CUMULUS CLOUDS at 2100ft.
